I work on making language model systems reliable and accountable in high-stakes settings, including education, workforce policy, and regulated finance. I am applying to PhD programs in the current admissions cycle.

**AI policy in higher education.** At the [Open Syllabus Project](https://www.opensyllabus.org/), I lead an analysis of how AI-use policies in academic syllabi have changed over time, working toward a public dashboard for students, universities, and journalists. I own this work end to end: I expanded the 33.5M-syllabus corpus behind it with 1.67M documents its crawlers had missed, and built automated quality checks that keep language model outputs valid and consistent across runs.

**Traceable skill extraction.** I am the first author of [LAiSER](/projects/laiser/), an open-source framework, under review at the _Journal of Open Source Software_, that links skills in job postings and syllabi to national taxonomies and records the source and similarity score of every match. I scaled its extraction pipeline to more than 1M job descriptions, reducing processing time from 2 months to 8 days on high-performance computing clusters. The framework is integrated into the MIT Digital Credentials Consortium's Credential Co-writer, and analysis built on it supported recommendations on two local training policies for the Texas Workforce Commission.

**Accountable autonomous agents.** I am a co-author of a manuscript submitted to _Software: Practice and Experience_ that systematizes threats and controls for autonomous agents in regulated financial systems, and I built its [open-source reference implementation](/projects/casebound/).

Beyond research, I have taught computer science and precalculus at a New York City charter high school, taught [workshops on multi-agent systems](/cv/) to 100+ students and faculty, served as a graduate assistant for Natural Language Processing, and co-organize Google Developer Groups DC. I hold an MS in Data Science from George Washington University (3.90 GPA).
